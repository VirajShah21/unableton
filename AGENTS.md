# Unableton

Unableton is an open-source, AI-first music editor for macOS. A prompt should create or refine real, editable music: tracks, notes, rhythm, sound settings, and arrangement. Superhuman defaults to AI composition and full inspection; Producer exposes manual editing. Users can keep multiple chats attached to one song.

## Product direction

- Keep the arrangement and chat visible together, with chat floating on the right using system Liquid Glass. The SwiftUI workspace reserves editing space with a safe-area inset and uses the system background extension to mirror arrangement colors behind chat. Keep the arrangement and lower editor in SwiftUI so their controls stay unobscured. Support resizing from the pane’s left edge and showing or hiding it with Control-Command-S. Let system materials adapt to appearance and accessibility settings. Use straightforward labels and native Mac controls, menus, keyboard shortcuts, Settings, and document windows.
- Superhuman is the first-launch default: AI composition with full inspection, no direct music editing controls. Producer retains the full editor. Keep mode window-owned and scene-restored; new windows use the last explicitly selected mode. Toolbar and View menu expose both modes (Control-Command-1/2). Mode never belongs to the musical document or changes its revision. Switching must preserve selection, conversations, capture, playback and AI generation; discard uncommitted manual field drafts.
- Keep track headers fixed on the left in one inset system Liquid Glass surface, without a shared border with the arrangement or separate glass cards for every row. Let scrolling clips and subtle semantic track-color bands supply the backdrop. Names and instruments stay visible; Superhuman shows mute/solo/arm and mix states as text, while Producer exposes their editing controls. Headers and clip lanes share one vertical scrolling surface so their rows remain aligned. The glass frame stays fixed in the window while row foregrounds and hit-testing are clipped to the visible track region.
- The left track surface extends behind the real native traffic lights. Compact Stop/Play/Record/Loop buttons share their horizontal row, in both modes and empty songs. Use a native navigation toolbar item without a separate glass background to align transport over the same track surface, ahead of the document title. Preserve AppKit's native window buttons and document delegate; never draw replicas or reparent them. `TitlebarInsetReader` only measures per-window chrome. One native scroll view owns lanes and track rows; explicitly clip row/lane foregrounds while letting the sidebar glass reach the titlebar.
- Extend only a decorative musical backdrop behind chat, never the entire interactive timeline: mirroring a native scroll view also mirrors its scrollbar. `ArrangementBackdropView` draws visible clip colors at the shared scrolling coordinates, without text, controls, hit testing or accessibility elements. Keep native horizontal and vertical indicators on the real scroller. The foreground background must respect safe-area edges so it does not cover the extended colors.
- Keep timeline zoom in a compact floating system-glass control at the arrangement's bottom-right, available in either mode and above the lower editor. Do not restore a permanent bottom status bar or redundant playback-status text. Keep export progress visible while rendering and other operation notices brief; errors remain actionable alerts or feature-local messages.
- Start the arrangement directly with its timeline ruler and pinned track list. Do not add an Arrangement title row or bar-count/time-signature summary, or relocate those removed labels elsewhere.
- Keep the timeline ruler pinned vertically and synchronized horizontally with the clips. It shares the existing native scroller, with a reserved ruler-height band, a matching visible-area mask and hit-test shape, and an adaptive background. Clip lanes and their decorative backdrop below this band; keep click-to-seek coordinates in musical content space. Do not add a second scroller to pin the ruler.
- Follow current Apple macOS design guidance. Use system-adaptive colors and materials. Preserve a clear musical canvas in both appearances and respect accessibility settings.
- Start with editable instrumental generation, acoustic and electronic instruments, recording/import, and WAV/MIDI export.
- Generated, recorded and imported vocals are audio clips. Singing generation uses optional bring-your-own-key providers, explicit monophonic melody notes and aligned lyric syllables. Transcribe converts a local solo whistle, hum, sung melody or beatbox take into editable notes. General cloud audio understanding, polyphonic and lyric transcription, third-party plugins, external MIDI recording, and advanced time stretching are later work.
- Never replace the real AI integration with canned genre arrangements or silently substitute a different model or instrument. Surface missing credentials, instruments, and unsupported capabilities clearly.

## Code ownership and boundaries

- `UnabletonCore`: serializable song models, instrument catalog, validated project commands, package snapshots, MIDI export, measured transcription events and constrained arrangement plans.
- Local `MusicalAnalyzer` reports belong to Core: exact entity diffs and bounded symbolic activity/pitch/pattern facts, never inferred listening judgments. `GenerationRecord.observation` is backward-compatible optional metadata; keep the format at version 1 and keep reports through Undo.
- `UnabletonLibrary`: app-wide managed downloads/imports, installation states, pinned artifact verification, atomic installation/removal and resource leases. No song mutations.
- `UnabletonAudio`: AVAudioEngine graphs, resolved samplers, transport, recording, local performance analysis, previews, and offline WAV rendering.
- `UnabletonDSP`: bounded C++ synthesis, event scheduling, and Objective-C++ audio-framework glue.
- `UnabletonAI`: OpenAI Responses client, tool schemas, local draft agent loop, constrained transcription arranger, vocal provider clients and run-scoped render cache, continuation items, and credential storage.
- `Unableton`: SwiftUI document scenes, feature views, session state, menus, and Settings. Keep the app entrypoint and root view small.

Manual controls and AI tools must use the same `ProjectCommand` layer. Add new musical editing capabilities there before exposing them through either interface. The shared parameter catalog defines names, units, limits, and defaults for both UI controls and AI tools.

Producer clip moves use direct canvas dragging, with a window-owned source identity, revision and pointer-down grab offset. `ClipDragGeometry` converts the fixed viewport to scrolling lane coordinates; `ClipDragContext` supplies one snapped placement for the actual clip preview and final shared `moveClip` command. Include the clip's one-point visual inset. Reject stale or incompatible destinations and select only after successful commit, without opening or changing the lower pane. Give dragging precedence over clicking while keeping ordinary clip selection available. Escape, view-mode changes, project revisions and gesture cancellation clear the preview and suppress the rest of that gesture. One bounded UI task handles edge scrolling and stops on release/cancel; it never schedules audio. Defer gesture-reset cleanup so it cannot race the release handler. Undo/Redo must keep a selected clip associated with its owning track.

Superhuman must hide manual editing affordances and prevent their commands, including stale callbacks, keyboard actions, drag/drop and accessibility actions. Keep inspection, selection, scrolling, copying and source navigation active; never blanket-disable the musical canvas. Guard the manual session entry point and helpers before side effects, without blocking AI commits. Playback loop on/off preserves the current range and is allowed; arbitrary loop-range edits and metronome settings remain Producer-only. Native Undo/Redo, save/export, recording/import and transcription insertion are also allowed in either mode. Capture creates required tracks through its own validated command transaction. Transcription generation choices remain available, but detected-event correction requires Producer. Do not introduce chat-attachment staging or audio understanding as part of view-mode work.

## State, persistence, and undo

- Each `.unableton` package owns its versioned song metadata, audio assets, conversations, and generation records. Keep credentials outside documents and Git.
- Window selection, pane state, and zoom are separate from persisted musical state. Only one project plays or records at a time.
- An AI run edits a draft against a captured revision. Commit successful work as one undo group. Cancellation, errors, and incomplete tool output must leave the live arrangement intact.
- Refresh project context every turn, including old chats. Queue runs per project. On a revision conflict, replan once; never overwrite concurrent manual edits.
- Undo musical changes while preserving conversation history and the record of what was undone.
- Retain audio needed by undo. Do not delete an asset just because its last visible clip was removed.
- Insert a transcription through shared commands as one undo step. The package keeps its source audio, reviewed transcript, recorded tempo and start position; transcription clips reference that source asset. Keep optional provenance fields backward compatible. Uninserted takes must survive project closure or replacement in Application Support/Unableton/RecoveredTakes, with metadata; report recovery failures.

## Audio rules

- Use one compiled musical schedule for live playback and offline export. UI timers display progress; they do not schedule notes.
- Never perform networking, file I/O, sample loading, allocation, blocking locks, actor hops, or UI work in a render callback.
- Build AVAudioNode tap blocks in an explicitly nonisolated factory with a Sendable closure. The legacy Objective-C block type does not prevent inherited MainActor isolation; Core Audio invokes it off-main. Regression tests must invoke the actual production block from a background executor, not only mocked tap setup.
- Prepare schedules, queues, and sample buffers ahead of rendering. Keep object destruction and graph changes off the audio thread.
- Stop, seek, loop, mute, and instrument changes must not leave stuck notes. Preserve release and effect tails in export.
- Ship no sampled sound banks and perform no startup/build downloads. Six procedural synth/electronic presets remain built in. The reviewed catalog is bundled metadata; compatible FreePats SF2 banks download separately to Application Support/Unableton/SoundLibrary. No implicit bundle/cache/environment fallback or silent substitution.
- Keep musical validation independent of installation. Stable IDs/versions/parameters survive save/reopen and Undo even when sounds are absent. Preflight using graph mute/solo rules; inject immutable InstrumentResources into every live/offline graph. Hold leases while playing/rendering/generating; removal cannot invalidate active work.
- Sound Library is the single workspace browsing/preview/download/selection surface, opened directly from the main toolbar and the instrument editor's Choose Sound action. Do not restore an Instruments popover or the workspace editor's instrument-selection picker. Transcribe's capture-specific layer choices remain separate. Management and previews are available in both modes; manual Use/Download & Use and Add Instrument Track/Download & Add Track remain Producer-only. A toolbar request with an instrument selected targets that track; a request with no track or an audio track selected explicitly creates a new instrument track without replacing audio. Preflight capacity and recheck mode, origin selection, revision and closure after preparation. Standalone browsing has no implicit song target. Expose individually accessible native action buttons and readouts. Explicit import accepts exact catalog hashes only and preserves originals. Download and install operations never enter musical Undo.
- The Sound Library uses native split navigation with a floating sidebar, adaptive sound grid and collapsible inspector. Keep Liquid Glass in navigation/controls, not sound tiles. Display-only names/families belong to SoundLibraryPresentation and must not change stable instrument IDs, sound parameters or serialized song data. Search original and curated names, descriptions, families, roles and source; clear hidden selections and stop their previews. Keep progress, failure/retry and request ownership visible in Downloads, and count shared banks separately from sound choices.
- Sound audition is explicit, local and installed-only. Generate short deterministic examples using the production renderer and exact preset defaults, valid supported pitches and explicit percussion maps. It never edits music or calls an AI provider. Pause ordinary song playback without resetting its position or automatically resuming. Block while capture is pending/active; new playback/capture invalidates audition before async work. Hold resource leases through render/playback, cancel stale renders and bound the temporary cache. Do not reuse a stop path that can finalize a microphone take.
- Library assignment has a named, weak originating project/track context; never infer a target from the latest focused window. Capture selection/revision at Use, then recheck track, mode, closure, selection and revision after installation. Explicit UI download/use authorizes only the shown dependency, with no redundant consent or automatic-download preference change. A canceled/stale assignment leaves completed downloads installed. Import results show all successes/failures; removal lists every shared sound affected.
- Downloads are HTTPS, pinned and bounded, at most two concurrent, deduplicated across windows, no automatic retries. Extract only known members into controlled staging, verify hashes and atomically install. Cancellation removes unowned jobs and partial staging; shared callers continue independently.
- Keep audited unrenderable legacy pitches distinct from document validity: preserve old notes/IDs in save/Undo/MIDI, expose warnings in the catalog, and report unsupported audible playback instead of silently omitting notes. Exclude newly audited incompatible banks; record evidence in docs/sound-library-audit.json.
- Missing sound banks are actionable dependencies, not permission to use undocumented Apple/GarageBand assets. Silent/Solo capture and original audio playback never resolve accompaniment; ordinary recording must offer an explicit without-accompaniment path.
- Request microphone access when recording is invoked. Monitoring starts off. Finalize or retain partial recordings after interruption.
- Transcription capture is separate from ordinary armed-track recording and leaves the song untouched until insertion. Bound takes and imports to 60 seconds, and imported files to 32 MiB. Keep the arrangement visible; support Listen to song, Silent timeline and Solo, with microphone monitoring off. Output muting must not mute capture. Analyze audio away from the main and render threads; no networking or model work belongs in capture callbacks.
- Configure the input node and tap before starting AVAudioEngine, including an empty song. Display actual received-input state, a level meter and elapsed capture time. Stop and report an explicit error if no input callbacks arrive; never report a zero-frame WAV as a successful recording. Keep input metering off the render thread.
- Rehearse uses the selected transcription audition without opening the microphone or requesting microphone permission. Main Play honors that audition while Transcribe is open; seeking and graph updates preserve its output mode. Normal playback started outside Transcribe uses normal song output. Follow playhead defaults on during rehearsal/capture, and active-clip indicators respect track mute/solo and the Solo audition mode.

## Transcription rules

- Whistle, Humming and Singing use local monophonic pitch/onset detection; Singing does not transcribe lyrics or synthesize a voice. Beatboxing uses reviewable onset and drum-class estimates. Confidence is a detector quality score, not a calibrated accuracy probability. Do not claim perfect transcription or silently fill uncertain passages with invented notes.
- Preserve measured event timing in seconds, including leading silence and rests. The default is Preserve Time and one performance layer, with no OpenAI request. Additional performance layers are deterministic copies on user-chosen compatible instruments; never transpose silently to satisfy a preset’s range.
- Timing and layers are chosen after recording. Preserve Time forbids timing changes. Minor Auto-time bounds each onset and duration change to ±0.25 beat. Major Auto-time fits the chosen section length. Every mode retains the reviewed source pitches, event count, velocities and order; melodic events remain monophonic.
- Only add matching drums, bass, harmony or countermelody when explicitly selected. Respect the song’s remaining 64-track capacity and preset pitch ranges. User-selected layers and instruments are authoritative.
- Optional AI arrangement receives measured notes and bounded song context, never the source recording. Its strict single-call tool may return source-event timing changes and the requested accompaniment layers only; it cannot change existing tracks, tempo, key or instruments. Validate the complete response on the host before constructing shared commands.
- Preview and insertion use the same validated draft and options. Recheck project revision and settings before inserting; replan once on a concurrent edit and retain the take after cancellation, failure or a second conflict. Keep AI usage, model routing and the applied summary in project history. See `docs/transcription.md`.
- Make the Transcribe flow explicit: choose a visible performance mode, Record, Stop & Transcribe, review, Add to Song. Keep progress, errors and next actions visible in the main pane at short editor heights; do not return to a generic initial empty state after a failure. Preview never commits. Add to Song may prepare selected AI options and then insert as one action; nothing is added simply by recording. Reveal successful additions in both timeline axes and provide Play in Song.

## AI rules

- Use the Responses API with strict, sequential function tools. Execute only complete arguments from completed responses.
- Expose project inspection, clip inspection, the instrument/parameter catalog, validated edits, and local rendered previews. When configured, expose singer discovery and score-to-singing rendering. Vocal tools derive trusted score data from an existing clip, never arbitrary model paths or audio URLs.
- Generate musical content and sound parameters rather than selecting canned songs. Musical transformations may reuse and develop phrases.
- Store API keys in a private, unencrypted local file by default in every build (`0700` directory, `0600` file), outside Git, app bundles, documents and logs. Settings must expose “Use macOS Keychain for Credentials”, off by default, for all providers. Only the new explicit boolean preference enables Keychain; old development preferences do not count as consent. Check that opt-in before every Keychain operation, including calls with an explicit backend. Never fall back to Keychain or move keys automatically when switching stores. New files use Application Support/Unableton/Credentials; keep an existing development file in place and show the active path in Settings. Use `store:false`; keep canonical conversations and required opaque continuation data locally.
- Enforce cancellation, elapsed-time and request budgets, retry limits, and tool-call deduplication. Explicit model selections stay fixed. Automatic mode starts with economical GPT-6 Luna and may restart once with GPT-6 Astra when capability is needed; expose the upgrade and its reason. Share the limits across both attempts and discard the unsuccessful draft.
- One `GenerationBudget` owns a prompt's monotonic five-minute active-work allowance, at most 32 model requests, 65,536 output tokens, two transient retries total, usage and upgrade state across all calls and the revision replan. Do not reset counters or restart Luna after an upgrade on a replan. Apply this to optional Transcribe arrangement too; offline transcription must not create network work.
- Only sound-download consent/installation pauses active generation time, with a cumulative 30-minute dependency-wait cap. Both composition and transcription use pause-aware watchdogs; never reset request/token/retry/upgrade/replan state. First AI download needs explicit app-wide automatic-download consent; dependency errors bypass model repair and escalation. Keep successful global downloads after draft cancellation/failure, and retain takes.
- Local analysis and Jev reviews are observation only and happen after a successful commit. Neither can reject, repair, escalate or mutate music, selection, Undo or composer continuation. Analyze immutable snapshots away from the main/audio threads; persist results against the generation ID, never whichever revision is current when a callback returns. Reviewer failure must not turn a successful generation into an error.
- Jev uses an independent `DraftReviewing`/`JevTransport` boundary, the official TypeSafe endpoint, pinned `jev-1.14.0`, opt-in preference and separate `typesafe` credential in the chosen store. Recheck opt-in after credential lookup before uploading. Send only bounded prompt/summary excerpts and allowlisted symbolic facts: no audio, asset metadata, paths or track names. A prompt may itself contain user-supplied text. Mark truncation and insufficient evidence honestly. Never forward opaque Responses history or claim the reviewer listened. Bound each observation to one request/eight seconds/no retries and at most two concurrent reviews per project. Keep observation requests separate from the composition budget and usage.
- Vocal generation is opt-in and uses a separate provider key in the selected credential store. Audial requires a user-supplied reference voice; never silently upload project recordings as references. Rinoa stock singers are experimental pending clarification of its public integration terms. See `docs/vocal-generation.md`.
- Stage generated bytes outside the live document. Preview must resolve draft audio, and only revision-validated success may install assets and commit. Retain committed audio through undo/redo and package it for offline reopening. Save the source score, lyric syllables, singer and provider with each take. Rendered audio does not follow later tempo or note edits.
- Never automatically retry a possibly billed vocal POST. Share completed score/voice renders across model upgrades and conflict replans within a prompt. Bound phrases to 60 seconds and 256 notes, four provider submissions and 64 MiB per prompt. Cancellation stops local work; accepted provider jobs may still use credits. Provider failures terminate the draft and must not cause a paid retry or model escalation.
- Audio level metrics are not listening. Do not claim the model heard a song without an implemented audio-input path.

## Build and verification

- `swift build` builds the SwiftPM executable and libraries.
- `swift test` runs the core, audio, and AI tests. Use filters for focused changes.
- `./script/build_and_run.sh` builds without network/sample downloads, stages `dist/Unableton.app`, and opens it. The Codex Run action uses this script.
- `./script/package_dmg.sh` builds a Release app at `dist/release/Unableton.app` and a versioned drag-to-Applications DMG in `dist/`, using `Resources/DMG/background.png`. It requires `create-dmg` and a graphical Finder session. `--app` wraps an existing app unchanged; `--output`, `--overwrite`, and `--help` control packaging. Default builds are ad-hoc signed development previews; Developer ID signing, notarization, and stapling remain separate release work. Verify the mounted layout in Finder and keep DMGs out of Git.
- Use meaningful tests for transactional edits, save/reopen, MIDI interoperability, render timing, non-silent audio, note termination, tool loops, and failure/cancellation behavior.
- Verify native UI with the connected computer-use surface. Browser instructions apply only if browser work is needed; this is a native app.
- Before release, validate the packaged app on macOS 26 and 27, keyboard focus, accessibility, recording permission states, device interruption, and real-key generation/refinement across multiple chats.
- Two physical microphone Whistle runs passed the full capture/preview/insertion flow using synthetic acoustic playback; see `docs/verification.md`. Complete human-performance acceptance in all four modes, accompaniment bleed, silent transport, denial/interruption recovery and timing against hardware before release. Synthetic fixtures do not establish real-performer accuracy.
